References

Annotated Bibliography

The following sources examine systems that connect visual information with haptic feedback. The sources include assistive navigation, sensory substitution, robotic teleoperation, and multimodal haptic generation.

[1] A Haptic Feedback System for Spatial Orientation in the Visually Impaired

Authors: Dvir Kleinberg, Roi Yozevitch, Ido Abekasis, Yuval Israel, and Eldad Holdengreber.

Publication: IEEE Sensors Letters, volume 7, number 9, 2023. DOI

Synopsis

This paper presents a portable assistive system that combines computer vision, ultrasonic distance sensing, machine learning, and haptic feedback for spatial orientation. It is the main assistive-navigation example used in this tutorial.

Reliability

High. The paper was published in a peer-reviewed IEEE journal and describes a technical implementation and evaluation.

[2] Haptics-based, Higher-order Sensory Substitution Designed for Object Negotiation in Blindness and Low Vision: Virtual Whiskers

Authors: Junchi Feng, Giles Hamilton-Fletcher, Todd E. Hudson, Mahya Beheshti, Maurizio Porfiri, and John-Ross Rizzo.

Publication: Disability and Rehabilitation: Assistive Technology, volume 20, number 5, 2025. Article

Synopsis

Virtual Whiskers uses modular vibration units to communicate traversable paths and obstacle depth. Its open-path and depth modes demonstrate how a vision system can encode navigation guidance rather than merely announce object categories.

Reliability

High to medium. The work reports user testing with people who are blind or have low vision and has a published assistive-technology article.

[3] Vis2Hap: Vision-based Haptic Rendering by Cross-modal Generation

Authors: Guanqun Cao, Jiaqi Jiang, Ningtao Mao, Danushka Bollegala, Min Li, and Shan Luo.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2023. The paper was accepted at ICRA 2023. Article

Synopsis

Vis2Hap generates haptic representations of surface properties such as roughness and slipperiness from visual input. It demonstrates that vision-to-haptic systems can communicate physical texture rather than only direction or obstacle proximity.

Reliability

High to medium. The work was accepted at a major robotics conference and includes a user study, although the freely available version is hosted on arXiv.

[4] Low-Cost Teleoperation with Haptic Feedback through Vision-based Tactile Sensors

Authors: Martina Lippi, Michael C. Welle, Maciej K. Wozniak, Andrea Gasparri, and Danica Kragic.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2024. Article

Synopsis

This paper presents a Tactile-to-Haptic framework in which camera-based tactile sensors estimate contact information and convert it into vibration for a human teleoperator. It provides an alternative example of visual sensing influencing haptic output.

Reliability

Medium. The paper describes experiments with real hardware, multiple objects, and multiple operators, but the available version is an arXiv preprint.

[5] VLH: Vision-Language-Haptics Foundation Model

Authors: Luis Francisco Moreno Fuentes, Muhammad Haris Khan, Miguel Altamirano Cabrera, Valerii Serpiva, Dmitri Iarchuk, Yara Mahmoud, Issatay Tokmurziyev, and Dzmitry Tsetserukov.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2025. Article

Synopsis

VLH connects visual perception, language instructions, robot actions, and generated haptic signals. It is used in this tutorial as a future direction showing how haptic feedback may become a context-aware output of a multimodal model.

Reliability

Medium. The paper includes a system implementation and experiments, but it is a recent preprint and its application is aerial robotics rather than assistive navigation.
