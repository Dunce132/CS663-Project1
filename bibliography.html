References

Annotated Bibliography

The following sources examine systems that connect visual information with haptic feedback. The sources include assistive navigation, sensory substitution, robotic teleoperation, and multimodal haptic generation.

[1] A Haptic Feedback System for Spatial Orientation in the Visually Impaired

Authors: Dvir Kleinberg, Roi Yozevitch, Ido Abekasis, Yuval Israel, and Eldad Holdengreber.

Publication: IEEE Sensors Letters, volume 7, number 9, 2023. DOI

Synopsis

This paper presents a portable assistive system that combines computer vision, ultrasonic distance sensing, machine learning, and haptic feedback for spatial orientation. It is the main assistive-navigation example used in this tutorial.

Reliability

This source is highly reliable for this tutorial because it was published in IEEE Sensors Letters, a scholarly engineering journal, and describes a complete assistive system that combines computer vision, ultrasonic sensing, machine learning, and haptic feedback. The paper is especially relevant because it connects visual and sensor measurements to spatial-orientation feedback rather than discussing object detection in isolation. Its main limitation is that the reported system and evaluation represent a particular prototype, so its results should not automatically be generalized to every environment, device configuration, or user population.

[2] Haptics-based, Higher-order Sensory Substitution Designed for Object Negotiation in Blindness and Low Vision: Virtual Whiskers

Authors: Junchi Feng, Giles Hamilton-Fletcher, Todd E. Hudson, Mahya Beheshti, Maurizio Porfiri, and John-Ross Rizzo.

Publication: Disability and Rehabilitation: Assistive Technology, volume 20, number 5, 2025. Article

Synopsis

Virtual Whiskers uses modular vibration units to communicate traversable paths and obstacle depth. Its open-path and depth modes demonstrate how a vision system can encode navigation guidance rather than merely announce object categories.

Reliability

This source is highly reliable for evaluating user-centered haptic navigation because it reports testing with people who are blind or have low vision and was published in a rehabilitation and assistive-technology journal. Its value is not limited to the sensing method; it also examines whether users can interpret different vibration modes while negotiating obstacles. The results are more meaningful than a purely simulated demonstration, but the findings remain limited by the study design, participant sample, training requirements, and the specific wearable hardware tested.

[3] Vis2Hap: Vision-based Haptic Rendering by Cross-modal Generation

Authors: Guanqun Cao, Jiaqi Jiang, Ningtao Mao, Danushka Bollegala, Min Li, and Shan Luo.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2023. The paper was accepted at ICRA 2023. Article

Synopsis

Vis2Hap generates haptic representations of surface properties such as roughness and slipperiness from visual input. It demonstrates that vision-to-haptic systems can communicate physical texture rather than only direction or obstacle proximity.

Reliability

This source is reasonably reliable because the work was accepted at the IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation and investigates its method through experiments and a user study. It is directly useful for showing that visual input can be converted into haptic descriptions of surface properties such as roughness and slipperiness, not only obstacle alerts. The available copy is hosted on arXiv rather than in a conventional journal archive, and the system focuses on haptic surface rendering rather than independent pedestrian navigation. Therefore, its conclusions should be applied to this tutorial by analogy rather than treated as direct evidence for every assistive-navigation scenario.

[4] Low-Cost Teleoperation with Haptic Feedback through Vision-based Tactile Sensors

Authors: Martina Lippi, Michael C. Welle, Maciej K. Wozniak, Andrea Gasparri, and Danica Kragic.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2024. Article

Synopsis

This paper presents a Tactile-to-Haptic framework in which camera-based tactile sensors estimate contact information and convert it into vibration for a human teleoperator. It provides an alternative example of visual sensing influencing haptic output.

Reliability

This source provides useful technical evidence because it describes experiments with real vision-based tactile hardware, multiple object interactions, and human teleoperators. It demonstrates a concrete pathway from camera measurements of contact deformation to haptic feedback, making it a strong comparison for the tutorial's vision-to-haptics pipeline. Its reliability is somewhat lower than a peer-reviewed journal article because the cited version is an arXiv preprint. In addition, the system is designed primarily for robot teleoperation, so its results do not directly establish that the same design would work for wearable navigation assistance.

[5] VLH: Vision-Language-Haptics Foundation Model

Authors: Luis Francisco Moreno Fuentes, Muhammad Haris Khan, Miguel Altamirano Cabrera, Valerii Serpiva, Dmitri Iarchuk, Yara Mahmoud, Issatay Tokmurziyev, and Dzmitry Tsetserukov.

Publication: arXiv preprint, 2025. Article

Synopsis

VLH connects visual perception, language instructions, robot actions, and generated haptic signals. It is used in this tutorial as a future direction showing how haptic feedback may become a context-aware output of a multimodal model.

Reliability

This source is useful for discussing emerging research because it presents an implemented multimodal architecture that connects visual observations, language instructions, robot actions, and generated haptic signals. It supports the tutorial's discussion of feedback that is selected according to context rather than produced by a single fixed rule. However, it is a recent arXiv preprint, so its claims should be treated as developing research rather than settled consensus. Its experiments focus on aerial robotics and virtual reality, not visually impaired pedestrian navigation, which limits how directly its results can be transferred to this project.
